Last summer I spent two weeks in a small fishing town on the northern coast, and it turned out to be the best holiday I have had in years. The harbour was quiet in the mornings, with only a few boats leaving before sunrise, and the smell of salt and fresh bread drifted through the narrow streets. Every afternoon I walked along the cliffs, where the path opened onto wide views of the sea and the weather changed every hour.

The people were genuinely welcoming. The owner of the guesthouse remembered my name, recommended a hidden beach, and refused to let me leave without a slice of her lemon cake. Local restaurants served simple food, mostly fish that had been caught that morning, and the prices were fair. Even the small museum, run by volunteers, was carefully arranged and surprisingly interesting.

What I liked most was how unhurried everything felt. Nobody rushed, the shops closed for lunch, and the evenings ended with long conversations on the pier. I came home rested, with a notebook full of ideas and a strong wish to return next year.
